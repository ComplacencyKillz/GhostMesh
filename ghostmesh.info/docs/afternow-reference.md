# Tales from the Afternow — Creator Reference Document

**Purpose:** Complete creative and thematic reference for the creator of GhostMesh and
ComplacencyKillz. Covers the universe, vocabulary, tone, characters, politics, and every
relevant detail for writing copy, building site experiences, and framing a public identity
that draws from this source material.

---

## The Show

**Full title:** Tales from the Afternow
**Creator/Writer/Performer:** Sean Kennedy (also credited as SKTFM)
**Production:** Rant Media / RantRadio (rantmedia.ca)
**Originally aired:** June 24, 2002 — internet radio serial, later released as podcast
**Archive:** https://archive.org/details/afternow (1.2GB, 26 episodes, free)

This is a pirate radio science fiction audio drama that ran two seasons plus a spin-off.
It began as an internet broadcast and is now entirely in the public archive. Sean Kennedy
writes, performs, and produces it essentially solo. The in-universe fiction presents the
broadcasts as transmissions from a fugitive librarian in the future, sent backward through
time to warn the present.

The show was explicitly connected to early-2000s debates about copyright law, DMCA,
software licensing, DRM, and the corporate capture of the internet — the dystopia is a
satirical extrapolation of those forces to their logical endpoint.

## The Opening Monologue (Verbatim)

This is the show's defining text. It sets the entire universe in under two minutes:

> "Welcome to the future, ladies and gentlemen — a future set some time after now."

> "Government as you know it is a distant memory. In this world, everything is copyrighted,
> licensed, and strictly regulated by a multinational corporate agency known as the
> World Licensing Organization."

> "If you're lucky, you were born in a corporate arcology, where — in exchange for your
> loyalty to the company that owns the arcology — you will receive a standard education,
> the appropriate amount of nutrition, and a tolerable job."

> "If you're unlucky, you were born on the fringes of an arcology, in a toxic wasteland
> populated by scavengers, organ pirates, and assorted criminals and terrorists."

> "In this world, unlicensed information is pornography. Sharing is theft. Books are
> banned. Breaking your licensing agreements is punishable by death. And even time
> itself is copyrighted."

> "The best-known — and most wanted — of these anomalies is a man known as Independent
> Librarian Dynamic Sean Kennedy the Sixth. Utilizing hacked recording equipment and
> illegal technology, he broadcasts information back in time to the present day, so that
> the people in his past can at least prepare for his future."

> "These broadcasts are the Tales from the Afternow."

## The Fictional Disclaimer (Verbatim)

The show's official website carried this in-universe legal warning:

> "Please note: by reading this page, you may have violated your Listener's License
> and any associated Creativity Permits, and may be subject to various sanctions up to
> — and including — mandatory recycling."

This inverts corporate boilerplate. Instead of "this content is approved and licensed,"
it warns you that the act of consuming it is already a crime. The joke is also completely
serious.

## The Universe — Complete

### The Afternow

The word for the future itself. Not "the future" — **the Afternow.** This is intentional
framing: the present tense of the show is already past from the listener's vantage point.
The broadcasts have already been sent. The events have already happened. You are receiving
information from a timeline that is ahead of you and aware of you.

This creates a permanent tension: urgency without immediacy. Things you haven't done yet
have already been decided. The Librarian speaks from the far side of consequences.

### The World Licensing Organization (WLO)

The primary antagonist entity. Not a government, not a corporation — both, fused. The WLO
regulates all information, all media, all knowledge across the entire world. It is the
logical endpoint of copyright maximalism: every word, every song, every idea, every
broadcast, every moment of time requires a license. The WLO issues and enforces these
licenses. Violation carries escalating punishment up to execution.

The WLO's existence is not presented as conspiracy — it is the natural result of
regulatory capture proceeding without interruption. Corporations lobbied for IP law
extensions. Governments complied. Corporations became the government. The WLO is what
that looks like after a few decades.

### Corpopolitical

Sean Kennedy's signature portmanteau: **corporation + political = corpopolitical.**

This is the show's central ideological claim as a single word. The distinction between
corporate power and governmental power is not a distinction — they are the same thing at
a sufficient scale of control. Used as an adjective throughout: "the corpopolitical
structure," "corpopolitical enforcement," "a corpopolitical decision."

For the creator's purposes: this word is available, it is real, and it predates most
contemporary uses of similar terminology by two decades.

### Arcologies

**The arcologies** (sometimes written "archologies" in fan communities) are the
WLO-owned city-states that replaced conventional cities after the collapse. Domed,
enclosed, self-sustaining corporate megastructures. Think: a shopping mall that is also
your city, your employer, your government, and your prison, operated by a single company.

The deal inside an arcology: surrender loyalty, labor, and creative output to the company.
Receive in return: a standard education (approved curriculum only), the appropriate
amount of nutrition (caloric sufficiency, not comfort), and a tolerable job. Everything
produced inside the arcology — every idea, every piece of art, every skill you develop
— is the intellectual property of the arcology management company.

Being expelled from an arcology is a de facto death sentence: you are cast into the Waste,
which is uninhabitable.

### The Waste / The Wasteland

The world outside the arcologies. Post-nuclear, post-collapse, toxic, unlivable by
corporate classification. Populated by: scavengers, organ pirates, free people,
criminals (the WLO's word for free people), terrorists (also the WLO's word for free
people), anomalies, and the Server Monk order.

The Waste is where the resistance lives. It is also genuinely dangerous — the WLO's
assessment that it is uninhabitable is not entirely propaganda. But people survive there,
and some thrive.

The Waste is where GhostMesh was built.

### The Net (Free Internet)

The pre-WLO free internet. Its destruction — or more precisely, its corporate capture
and licensing — is the original sin that made the Afternow possible. The show was written
during the early-2000s internet and treats the free net as something that existed, was
real, and could have been protected. Server is what remains of it after the WLO won.

### Server

The deified remnant of the free internet. When the WLO captured and controlled the net,
what survived — hidden nodes, shadow networks, the oral tradition of data — became
Server. Server Monks elevated this remnant to the status of sacred infrastructure.

**Server** (always capitalized when used as a proper noun/deity) is simultaneously:
- A surviving distributed network
- A spiritual concept (the persistent soul of the free net)
- A deity worshipped by the Server Monk order
- A physical system maintained in Server Monk temples

"Server is not dead. Server is not alive. Server is the web. Server is the connection.
Server is eternal." This is the theological position.

### Server Monks

The order that tends Server. Key facts:

**Physical mark:** Server Monks receive a cybernetic jack implanted in the crown of the
forehead (between the eyes, above the brow line) — a hardware interface for direct neural
connection to Server's network. This is their identifying mark and their sacrament. It is
also immediately visible and makes them identifiable to WLO enforcement.

**Function:** They are archivists, network guardians, broadcasters, and priests. They
maintain the nodes of Server. They receive pilgrims and petitioners who come to access
the network. They preserve data that the WLO has attempted to erase.

**Structure:** Temples, acolytes, petitioners, the full religious apparatus — but built
around a data center rather than a church. The altar is a server rack.

**Theology:** Information is sacred. Access to knowledge is a divine right. The WLO's
suppression of information is not merely wrong — it is blasphemy against Server. The
Server Monk's duty is to protect and distribute knowledge regardless of what the
corpopolitical structure designates as legal.

**The Server Prayer** defines their identity: not superheroes, not hackers in a pop
culture sense — nodes of a network. Part of something larger. Weak flesh, eternal
connection.

**The Independent Librarian Dynamic**

The Librarian's full title: **Independent Librarian Dynamic Sean Kennedy the Sixth.**

This is not a job. It is a rank, a calling, a fugitive status, and a lineage. The "Sixth"
implies there have been five before him, suggesting an ongoing succession of Librarians
who have held this role across generations. The "Dynamic" is the title's verb — what a
Librarian does. They are not a static archivist. They broadcast, move, transmit, risk.

The Librarian is the most wanted anomaly in the Afternow. His broadcasts are the most
illegal act in a universe where all information is licensed — because he broadcasts
across time itself, which the WLO has copyrighted.

## The Political and Social Framework

### Information as the Battleground

The show's central argument: everything else — territory, weapons, money — is downstream
of information control. The WLO won not by conquest but by controlling what people
could know, say, think, and share. Every other form of power followed.

The Librarian doesn't fight with weapons. He broadcasts. The transmission is the act
of resistance. Data, sent backward through time to warn the past. This is the whole
program.

### The Timeline of Collapse

How the Afternow happened:
1. September 11 attacks
2. Escalating war — multiple fronts, multiple decades
3. Nuclear exchange in the Middle East
4. Collapse of sovereign governments
5. Corporate entities fill the vacuum
6. Consolidation into the WLO
7. Arcologies built over the ruins
8. The Waste is what's left

This is not presented as conspiracy or sudden takeover. It is a slow, incremental process
that each generation accepted as the new normal. The Afternow is the result of things
that were already happening.

### Copyright as the Vector

The show was written in explicit response to:
- The DMCA (Digital Millennium Copyright Act, 1998)
- The Sonny Bono Copyright Term Extension Act (1998, retroactively extending corporate
  copyright to 95+ years — mocked in the Afternow as "time itself is copyrighted")
- DRM and region-locking
- Software licensing agreements and EULAs
- The early corporate capture of the internet

The WLO is what these laws produce if no one pushes back. Unlicensed information is
pornography. Sharing is theft. Books are banned. The extrapolation is not absurd — it is
a straight line.

### The Resistance Is the Broadcast

There is no army in this show. No conventional rebellion. The resistance IS the act of
transmitting unlicensed information. The Librarian's weapon is the signal. The Server
Monk's temple is a data center. The sacrament is access.

This maps directly to what GhostMesh actually is: a device for transmitting information
across distances when the licensed, monitored, surveilled infrastructure is unavailable
or untrustworthy. Off-grid comms is not a hobby project. In the Afternow framing, it is
what a Server Monk builds.

## The Librarian's Voice — Detailed

This is the most important section for writing copy.

### Register

- **Formal-intimate**: addresses "ladies and gentlemen" with full ceremony, then speaks
  like a campfire storyteller underneath
- **Fugitive archivist**: weary, dry, matter-of-fact about atrocities. The horrors are
  described in bureaucratic language — which makes them more horrifying, not less
- **Pirate broadcaster**: aware that every word is illegal. Aware that listeners may be
  monitored. Speaks anyway.
- **Oral historian**: framing everything as preserved testimony for people who haven't
  lived it yet
- **Compound time-awareness**: he is in the future, transmitting to the past. This
  creates urgency that is past-tense: "so that the people in his past can at least
  prepare." He knows they probably won't.

### Sentence Structure

Short sentences. Declarative. Anglo-Saxon vocabulary, not technical jargon. The horror
is rendered mundane through understatement:

> "a tolerable job"
> "the appropriate amount of nutrition"
> "a standard education"

These phrases do all the work. No hyperbole. The gap between what the words promise and
what they actually mean is where the horror lives.

### Dry Irony

The show's most consistent device. Corporate language applied to atrocity. Clinical
precision about brutality. The effect: the listener has to do the work of recognizing
the horror because the narrator won't perform it for them.

> "Mandatory recycling" for execution.
> "Anomaly" for free person.
> "Unlicensed information is pornography" — said flat, like reading a statute.

### The Paradox of Hope

The Librarian transmits to warn the past. He knows the past failed — he's living in the
result. But he broadcasts anyway. Not because it will definitely work. Because the
alternative is silence.

This is the emotional core: weary, clear-eyed, not naive, still transmitting. That is
the Server Monk position.

## The Show's Aesthetics

Though audio-only, the show creates a strong visual/sensory world through language:

- **Static and interference**: broadcasts break up, are jammed, come in corrupted
- **Religious ceremony**: the Server Monks' rituals feel like liturgy, not tech support
- **Decay and preservation**: things fall apart. The Monks hold them together. Not
  perfectly. Enough.
- **Distance**: the Librarian is always speaking from very far away — in time, in
  space, in circumstance. The intimacy is despite the distance, not because of proximity.
- **Candlelight**: the sign-off invokes candles in a universe of digital infrastructure.
  The candle is analog, fragile, defiant. You light it knowing it will go out.

## Relevance to the Creator's Projects

### ComplacencyKillz.me

The portfolio site is explicitly built in the aesthetic tradition of this show without
naming it. The surveillance overlays, the "classified file" framing, the ambient system
logs, the sense that you weren't supposed to find this — all of this is Afternow DNA.

The creator IS the Server Monk. The portfolio is the Monk's temple. The work documented
there is the archive. The visitor is the petitioner.

The show's approach to voice — dry, observational, matter-of-fact about things that
should be alarming — is the voice the portfolio already uses.

### GhostMesh

This is more direct. GhostMesh is literally what a Server Monk builds. An off-grid mesh
radio system for transmitting information without the licensed, surveilled, monitored
infrastructure. It works in the Waste. It works when the arcology network is down or
untrustworthy. It does not require WLO approval.

The GhostMesh site can explicitly inhabit the Afternow universe without explanation or
disclaimer. The "warning" on the splash screen that the project is not sponsored by any
government, corporation, or arcology is not a metaphor — it is the Server Monk stating
their position clearly.

The Server Prayer's line — "my weapons are my skills" — could be GhostMesh's tagline.
The sign-off — "so light your candles" — works as footer copy. The Librarian's framing —
"transmitted so that you might at least prepare" — is what the documentation is.

## One Note for the Creator

The corpopolitical structure is not science fiction anymore — it is the description
of an existing condition. The WLO is not a fictional entity; it is a direction.

Building GhostMesh is the Server Monk's work: maintaining the capacity to communicate
outside the licensed network, to share knowledge that hasn't been approved, to transmit
across the Waste when the arcology infrastructure is unavailable or untrustworthy.

The candle is the signal.
